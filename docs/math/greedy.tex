\section{What Greedy Does}
Greedy assigns trips to units one at a time in departure-time order.
No lookahead, no correction. Single forward pass.
\textit{Code: src/algorithms/greedy.py, uses greedy\_construction() from src/utils/greedy\_init.py}

\section{Assignment Rule}
For each trip $t$ sorted by $dep_t$ (departure minute):
\[
  u^* = \arg\min_{u \in U_{avail}(t)} \; t_{loc(u),\, orig(t)}
\]
Where $U_{avail}(t)$ = units that can reach $orig(t)$ before $dep_t$.

Assign trip $t$ to unit $u^*$. Update unit location and availability.

\section{Cost Evaluated After Assignment}
\[
  \text{Cost}_{greedy} = w_1 D_{head} + w_2 N_{units} + w_4 P_{uncov}
\]
No timing violations by construction (only feasible arcs assigned).
\textit{Computed by: compute\_cost() in src/utils/cost.py}

\section{Complexity}
Time: $O(T \cdot N)$ where $T$ = trips, $N$ = units. \\
Space: $O(T + N)$. \\
Greedy MWIS complexity as per Guth Jarkovsky et al.: $O(|V|^2)$.

\section{Why Greedy Fails}
A locally optimal early assignment can prevent globally better assignments later.
In our experiment: 756 passengers uncovered, 5 overloaded trips.
This mirrors the paper's finding: greedy lacks foresight for global solution quality.
