\section{What SA Does}
SA starts from the greedy solution and iteratively applies small random
moves, accepting improvements always and worse solutions with decreasing probability.
\textit{Code: src/algorithms/simulated\_annealing.py, function solve\_simulated\_annealing()}

\section{Acceptance Rule (Metropolis Criterion)}
\[
  P(\text{accept worse move}) = e^{-\Delta C / T_k}
\]
Where $\Delta C = C_{new} - C_{current} > 0$ and $T_k$ is current temperature.
\textit{Used in: solve\_simulated\_annealing() acceptance block}

\section{Cooling Schedule}
\[
  T_{k+1} = T_k \cdot r
\]
Parameters in code: $T_0 = 500$, $r = 0.995$, $T_{min} = 0.5$, 40 iterations per temperature.
\textit{Defined as: initial\_temp, cooling\_rate, min\_temp in solve\_simulated\_annealing()}

\section{Neighbourhood Moves}
Three moves applied randomly at each iteration:

\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Move & What it does & Code function \\
\midrule
Relocate & Move one trip from unit $u_1$ to unit $u_2$ & move\_relocate() \\
Swap & Exchange one trip between two units & move\_swap() \\
Couple/Decouple & Add or remove a second unit on a trip & move\_couple\_decouple() \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\section{Objective}
Same cost function as Greedy:
\[
  \text{Cost} = w_1 D_{head} + w_2 N_{units} + w_3 V_{maint} + w_4 P_{uncov} + w_5 V_{time}
\]
\textit{Computed by: compute\_cost() after every accepted move}

\section{Result in Our Experiment}
SA reduced cost from 5,696.9 (greedy) to 4,074.1 -- a 28.5\% improvement.
Zero timing violations. This is the best classical result.
SA is analogous to classical annealing; QAOA in the paper is its quantum equivalent.
