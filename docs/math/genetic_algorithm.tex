\section{What GA Does}
GA maintains a population of 80 schedules (chromosomes) and evolves them
over up to 300 generations using selection, crossover, and mutation.
\textit{Code: src/algorithms/genetic\_algorithm.py, function solve\_genetic\_algorithm()}

\section{Chromosome Encoding}
A chromosome is a list of $T$ integers (one per trip):
\[
  C = [u_1, u_2, \ldots, u_T], \quad u_i \in \{0, 1, \ldots, N-1\}
\]
$u_i$ = index of the unit assigned to trip $i$ (trips sorted by departure\_min).
\textit{Built in: solve\_genetic\_algorithm() population initialization}

\section{Fitness Function}
\[
  fitness(C) = \text{Cost}(C) + 10 \cdot w_5 \cdot V_{time}(C)
\]
Lower is better. Timing violations penalized 10x extra.
\textit{Computed by: compute\_cost() then penalty applied in evaluate loop}

\section{Selection}
Tournament selection with $k=5$: pick 5 random chromosomes, keep the best.
\textit{Code: tournament\_select() inside solve\_genetic\_algorithm()}

\section{Crossover (PMX, probability 0.85)}
Take two cut points $a < b$. Swap genes between positions $a$ and $b$:
\[
  Child_1[a:b] = Parent_2[a:b], \quad Child_1[\text{rest}] = Parent_1[\text{rest}]
\]
After crossover: call repair\_chromosome() to fix infeasible assignments.
\textit{Code: crossover block + repair\_chromosome() from src/utils/feasibility.py}

\section{Mutation (probability 0.10 per gene)}
Randomly replace a gene with a different valid unit index.
\textit{Code: mutation block in solve\_genetic\_algorithm()}

\section{Elitism}
Top 2 chromosomes always survive to next generation.
\textit{Code: elitism copy at start of each generation loop}

\section{Why GA Underperforms Here}
Crossover breaks physical continuity -- unit assigned in Parent A is now
at a different location due to Parent B assignments. The result is
trains that teleport between stations. Repair fixes some but not all.
Cost at 60 trips: 7,910.6 (worse than greedy at 5,696.9).
